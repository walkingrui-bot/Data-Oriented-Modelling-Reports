# CG-021 protocol

## Question
When a self-generated world model is given excess candidate capacity but no discrete hypothesis-selection rule, how does the candidate population organize itself, and how does new evidence enter that population?

## Data
The exact synthetic panel from CG-017—020 is reused: 600 parameter groups split 400/100/100 for train/dev/test. Each group contains three observationally equivalent linear-Gaussian causal worlds, each represented with support/no-support variants. The test set contains 100 independent parameter groups and 600 task instances.

## Local probe and generative scale
The three-variable panel and four world updates are a controlled mechanistic probe. They are training and scoring windows on a generative-state process, not a definition that the underlying problem is a four-step or short-sequence task. The learned state is explicitly iterable and the research target is the organization of the generated statistical world.

## Model
Both variants use a 32-dimensional token space, 4-head attention, 64-dimensional feed-forward blocks, zero dropout and three untied attention blocks per world update. Persistent state is `(B_t, logits_t)` with K candidate 3×3 relation matrices and K mixture logits. Relation matrices have zero diagonal and row absolute sum at most 0.95.

Candidate capacity:

- `pool3`: K=3, 26,764 parameters.
- `pool8`: K=8, 26,924 parameters.

The five extra pool8 candidate seeds are unlabeled capacity only. They are not assigned roles or true worlds.

## No preset selection
Training contains no discrete candidate-management operation. Specifically:

- no KEEP/DROP/BRANCH action;
- no top-k pruning;
- no winner-take-all gate;
- no candidate-to-ground-truth assignment;
- no entropy, sparsity or diversity target;
- no intermediate B supervision.

All K candidates persist through all four world updates. Softmax weights are generated by the model and remain continuous.

## Evidence staging
For every task, observational covariance is available throughout. For support-present tasks, support response and support target are masked during updates 1–2 and revealed from update 3 onward. Query target remains excluded from world generation and is used only when reading the final simulated consequence.

## Training
Seeds 11 and 22. 450 Adam updates, batch size 64, learning rate 0.0015, gradient clipping 5. Development NLL checked every 50 steps; best checkpoint retained. Only final mixture NLL is supervised.

## Measurements
Before evidence (`t=2`) and after final revision (`t=4`), record:

- mixture entropy and effective number of candidates `exp(H)`;
- top candidate weight;
- total-variation change in candidate weights;
- RMS change in relation matrices;
- weighted pairwise relation diversity;
- weighted and best-candidate distance to the true SCM (evaluation only);
- original-query and all-three-query consequence NLL.

## Controls

### Post-hoc pruning
For frozen pool8 checkpoints, evaluate the intact eight-candidate mixture, the three highest-weight candidates renormalized, and every one of the 56 possible three-candidate subsets. This pruning is evaluation-only and never enters training.

### Evidence pulse
Reveal support evidence only at update 3, remove it again at update 4, and compare with never-revealed and continuously revealed runs. Measure relation-state imprint at update 3 and its persistence after evidence removal.

### Wrong-support transplant
Within each observationally equivalent parameter group, replace a support-present task's support observation with that of another true world while keeping the receiver's observational covariance and query. Measure prediction displacement and relative fit to receiver vs donor consequence.

## Independent unit
Group-level comparisons aggregate the six repeated task instances within each parameter group and average the two seeds. Bootstrap intervals resample the 100 independent parameter groups 10,000 times.
