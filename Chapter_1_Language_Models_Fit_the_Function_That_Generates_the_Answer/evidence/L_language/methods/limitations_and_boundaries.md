# Limitations and current boundaries

- Most language-geometry experiments are exploratory pilots built from small or curated corpora. They establish candidate invariants, not population-level psycholinguistic laws.
- Character-bigram cross-language experiments overstate some bridge effects because phrase morphology and orthographic recurrence contribute. Word-level reruns survive, but the incremental bridge gain becomes much smaller.
- The strongest cross-language signal localizes to local–mesoscale organization. The experiments do **not** establish a mysterious hundreds-token global geometry.
- Classical→modern and multilingual relay transformations include manually constructed/checked versions; independent human parallel corpora should replace them for publication-grade claims.
- The OneStop eye pilot uses only the Scanpath Studio demo subset (2 readers). It is valuable for mechanism falsification and pipeline development, not for final effect sizes.
- Surface gaze trajectory replay was negative. “Packet reinstatement” must therefore refer to an internal relational state, not literal replay of eye movements.
- The six-style pilot contains two reporting inconsistencies retained in the correction log; those exact style fractions require rerun before publication.
- The sensorimotor-coordination interpretation is a mechanistic hypothesis. The language and gaze evidence is convergent but does not by itself prove that abstract reasoning and peripheral sensorimotor control are neurally identical.
- Learning-dynamics proxies (BP/homeostasis, STDP, TRF) are exploratory. Strong sequence learning alone does not guarantee occurrence-role binding or structural-language qualification.
