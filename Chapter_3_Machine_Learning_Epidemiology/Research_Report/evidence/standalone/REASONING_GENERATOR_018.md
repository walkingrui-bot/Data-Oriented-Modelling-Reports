# REASONING-GENERATOR-018

## Question

After the lower-level language operator algebra has been identified, does a reasoning trajectory reduce to repeated composition/feedback of those existing operators, or can reasoning contain additional reproducible low-dimensional generators?

Two explicit competing hypotheses are tested:

H0: reasoning = closure of the language algebra.

H1: reasoning = language closure + a small additional generator subspace.

This is a controlled diagnostic assay. It validates the mathematical test and its causal interpretation; it does not yet establish that a natural LLM necessarily contains a separate reasoning-generator subspace.

## Language algebra

State dimension is 8. Six visible language actions are affine operators. Their displacement vectors are constructed to lie exactly in one common 3D language-movement subspace. The operators are learned from 15,000 independent one-step language transitions.

Maximum parameter reconstruction error is 4.410e-15.

Thus the estimated language algebra is effectively exact before any reasoning analysis.

## Negative control: reasoning as pure language closure

A reasoning-length sequence generated only by repeated language-operator composition is predicted using the fitted operators.

Maximum closure residual is 2.431e-14.

The assay therefore does not manufacture a "reasoning generator" when the trajectory is actually generated by language closure alone.

## Positive controlled reasoning case

The identical language actions are used, but the closed-loop reasoning trajectory receives two additional state-dependent low-rank updates at reasoning stages 3 and 6. No new visible language operator is introduced.

After subtracting the fitted language closure, residual energy appears only at those reasoning stages.

Residual SVD:
- mode 1 energy = 0.6992
- top-2 energy = 1.000000
- stable rank = 1.4302

The recovered 2D residual subspace matches the true injected generator subspace to principal-angle error 0.000e+00° / 0.000e+00°.

Relative to the pre-existing 3D language-movement subspace, the two reasoning directions sit at principal angles:
49.458° and 78.463°.

Thus the controlled reasoning layer shows the structure suggested by LANGUAGE-HIERARCHICAL-GENERATORS-017: one new mode partially reuses lower-level language directions, while the second opens largely outside them.

## Cross-task reproducibility

The reasoning cases are partitioned into four disjoint task groups based on visible token-pattern structure. The top-two residual subspace is independently estimated inside each group.

Every task group places 100% of residual energy in its top two modes and recovers the same generator plane to numerical precision. Pairwise principal angles among group-specific generator planes are also at numerical precision.

The residual is therefore a reproducible shared generator geometry, not an idiosyncratic trajectory error.

## Functional intervention

Half of the reasoning cases are used to estimate the two residual generators as state-dependent rank-1 update maps. They are then applied to an entirely held-out half.

Using language closure alone gives held-out answer accuracy 0.8520 relative to the true reasoning system.

Restoring both learned reasoning generators gives 1.0000 answer accuracy and reconstructs final state/logit to numerical precision.

Across the full main sample, the true reasoning system and the pure language-closure rollout differ in hard answer on 14.71% of cases.

This provides the functional criterion: the residual subspace is not merely descriptive. Removing it changes reasoning outcomes; learning it from one subset and restoring it rescues held-out reasoning.

## Twenty random-orientation replications

The complete experiment is repeated with 20 independently rotated language/reasoning geometries.

Mean residual mode-1 energy = 0.7070.
Mean residual stable rank = 1.4147.
Mean language-closure answer accuracy relative to the reasoning teacher = 0.8383.
The learned-generator model achieves 1.0000 held-out answer accuracy in every replication.

Thus the assay consistently distinguishes:
1. pure algebraic closure, where no residual generator is found;
2. a reasoning layer with a small additional generator subspace, where the subspace is recovered and functionally necessary.

## Working mathematical object

Given learned language operators T_a, define the language-closure prediction

x_hat_(t+1) = T_(a_t)(x_t)

and the reasoning residual

r_t = x_(t+1) - x_hat_(t+1).

Stacked residuals form R. A reasoning-generator candidate is supported when:

1. R has structured low-rank energy above the closure-control residual;
2. its leading subspace replicates across tasks;
3. it generalizes as a state-dependent update on held-out reasoning cases;
4. ablation changes reasoning while leaving the language algebra itself intact.

In the positive controlled case,

G_reason = <G_language>_(composition, feedback) + R_reason

with effective residual stable rank ≈ 1.43.

The result validates a practical route from the language control algebra to a mathematical reasoning-generator assay. The next empirical step is to apply exactly this closure/residual/surgery test to a genuinely trained self-generated small language model, where whether R_reason is nonzero is not specified by construction.
