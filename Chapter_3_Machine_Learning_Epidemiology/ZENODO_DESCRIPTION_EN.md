# Machine Learning Epidemiology and Case Dissection: Training History, Predictive-State Geometry, and Local Control — Chapter 3, with The Engineer’s Edition

**Archival snapshot: research v1.9 + engineering v0.6 · 26 September 2026**

This archive preserves version 1.9 of a living experimental research program on how training history, data constraints, and model capacity shape predictive states and answer-producing dynamics in trained neural systems. It also preserves the companion Model Control Diagnostics report, Chapter 3 — The Engineer’s Edition, version 0.6. These are two complementary parts of Chapter 3, consolidated in one archival snapshot. Later revisions of either living record retain their own version and citation context.

The research program’s central object is the model’s future-response function: how the current computational state changes the distribution and controllability of subsequent states, outputs, and responses to intervention. The program combines controlled neural interventions, predictive-state operators, local Jacobian and spectral measurements, and exactly enumerable reasoning systems to connect learning history with measurable functional structure.

The experiments establish several linked observations within their tested settings. Matched training interventions show that learning order can alter continuous response fields even when visible outputs remain matched, that optimizer state can carry history into subsequent learning updates, and that changes in training material can alter held-out predictive behavior. In controlled teacher–student experiments, graded probability or margin information can recover known teacher control geometry across the tested miniature architectures. Local control measurements repeatedly show that a small number of dominant modes can carry a large fraction of future-response energy. Additional experiments identify multiple internally distinct states that support the same apparent answer readiness. Calibrated constructions distinguish information that is merely readable from internal state from information that is causally used by downstream output. Exact finite-state constructions and statistical analyses of independent text segments provide complementary tests of predictive-state sufficiency, functional equivalence, and state-dependent control.

A dedicated model-scale study examines 30 GRUs trained in a shared 400-case reasoning world, varying hidden width and training coverage with two seeds per condition. Model width strongly correlates with PCA-whitened separation between states assigned different answers, with within-coverage Spearman correlations ranging from 0.960 to 0.985. Across the same models, covariance-weighted answer-gradient stable rank is approximately 1.4–2.7; local first-order margin radii vary non-monotonically with width. These measurements support a geometric account in this controlled world in which additional capacity primarily increases separation among predictive states and local answer control is concentrated in comparatively few directions. Training coverage is strongly associated with full-world accuracy. Internal predictive-state geometry continues to change after accuracy has saturated. Connections to reduced functional interference and scaling-related capability transitions are retained as experimentally motivated hypotheses for subsequent causal testing.

The Engineer’s Edition connects nine cloud experiments with subsequent local model measurements and evidence updates. It develops a practical workflow for state-conditioned token diagnostics, executable operator queries, tool-selection scores, and host-side execution contracts. A frozen 897-parameter GRU supplies finite-intervention calibration; a 3,520-cell token–state study measures context dependence; and an included recovered runtime executes a 6,336-transition operator atlas. The supplied local reports document an internal-state intervention that restores the correct tool to first place in one selected SmolLM2-135M-Instruct case. In a separate frozen 100-task BFCL comparison, a 22.7M relevance ranker and TF-IDF matching each obtain 99/100. A forty-task paired paraphrase study reports 38/40 and 34/40 respectively, with an exact paired test of p = 0.125. The frozen acceptance policy’s coverage changes from 30% on the original requests to 5% on their paraphrases. The report gives accuracy, intervention outcomes, and accepted coverage their own definitions and denominators, connecting the observed results to explicit engineering interfaces.

The research component contains a 147-page English report, 38 original experiment archives, included code and data, figures, and two completed replication and validation reports with accompanying reviews and audits. The engineering component adds a 57-page English report, all 229 expanded members of its supplied cloud evidence archive, eleven original engineering stage bundles, and the supplied local reports and usage documents. The eleven stage bundles preserve successive experiment records and are not a count of independent replications. Together, the two components include claim-status records, deterministic numerical checks, provenance records, checksums, and file-level evidence indexes. The source maps identify materials included in this snapshot and companion records referenced by the reports; referenced local implementations, raw scores, protocols, and checkpoints are included only where the inventory explicitly marks them as present. These materials provide a traceable experimental basis for reproducing, auditing, challenging, and extending the reported results.
